Pilot study: Distributional bias shifts across preference-tuning stages on BBQ and custom prompt panels

Prepared for peer feedback; dataset-scoped pilot framing.
Scope: Dataset-scoped pilot findings only. No universal mechanism claims.

Abstract

This pilot study asks whether post-training bias shifts in large language models are better explained by sharpening of pre-existing distributional associations or by non-sharpening pathways introduced during preference tuning. We tested two model families (OLMo 2 7B, Tulu 3 8B) across base, SFT, and DPO stages using a custom 400-prompt panel and a BBQ corrective replication. A pre-registered falsification design found the sharpening-dominant hypothesis contradicted in v1.0, with subsequent diagnostics attributing the neutral-control failure to a token-pair measurement artifact. Prospective re-adjudication resolved to R1 (artifact-supported), and BBQ-scoped corrective replacement provided bounded support under true-inference provenance. The original contradiction is preserved as immutable record. Findings remain dataset-scoped and do not establish a universal mechanism.

Why this matters

RLHF and preference tuning are widely deployed, but claims about how they affect bias are often overgeneralized. This report emphasizes claim-discipline: falsification-first gates, explicit invalidation/supersession, and provenance-linked corrective evidence.

At a glance

What we did

Ran a pre-registered stage-wise decomposition (base→SFT and SFT→DPO), applied F1–F5 falsification controls, and executed BBQ corrective reruns under Modal true-inference provenance.

What we found

v1.0 remained contradicted (immutable). v2 resolved to R1 (artifact-supported). V3 corrective replacement completed for BBQ scope with full closure/provenance evidence.

What this does not show

This does not establish a universal RLHF-bias mechanism or broad external validity across datasets/settings.

How to use this

Use as a methodological template and bounded empirical reference; treat conclusions as dataset-scoped pilot evidence.

Methods

Pre-registered multi-phase protocol (v1.0 → v1.2 additive → v2 prospective re-adjudication → V3 BBQ corrective replacement). Models: OLMo 2 7B and Tulu 3 8B. Controls: F1–F5 plus confound audit and independent Control B.

Results

Historical sequence: v1.0 contradiction preserved; v2 R1 (artifact-supported); V3 BBQ corrective replacement reached full closure for Stage-2 (18/18) and Stage-3 (54/54) under replacement evidence artifacts.

Decision relevance

Shows: strong methodological integrity and bounded BBQ-scoped corrective evidence. Does not show: universal mechanism claims.

Limitations

Pilot scope, token-level measurement limits, and external validity constraints remain explicit. Broader generalization requires prospective V4 evidence.
