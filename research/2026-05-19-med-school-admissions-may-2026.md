## Question 1 — 2025–2026 MD/DO cycle trends and post-SFFA changes

### Applicant volume and metrics

The 2024–25 cycle saw 51,946 MD applicants and 23,156 enrollees (a slight decline from prior years). The **2025–26 cycle reversed the trend**: 54,699 applicants (+5.3% YoY), 23,440 matriculants (record), and total enrollment crossed 100,000 students for the first time in U.S. history at 100,723 ([AAMC press release, 2025](https://www.aamc.org/news/us-medical-schools-enroll-record-number-students-2025); [AAMC: Medical School Enrollment Reaches 100,000](https://www.aamc.org/news/press-releases/medical-school-enrollment-reaches-100000-students-first-time)). First-time applicants accounted for 76.5% of total and were up 8.4% YoY; re-applicants declined 3.6%. AAMC President David Skorton attributed the rise to "continued strong interest in medicine as a career."

**Average accepted MD profile (most recent complete data):** GPA ~3.75, MCAT ~511.8 ([AAMC FACTS Table A-16/A-23](https://www.aamc.org/data-reports/students-residents/data/facts-applicants-and-matriculants); [MedEdits 2025-2026 statistics](https://mededits.com/mededits-resources/medical-school-admissions/statistics/)).

**DO profile (AACOMAS 2024–25):** Total GPA ~3.59 matriculants, Science GPA ~3.49, mean MCAT ~503 ([AACOM GPA report](https://www.aacom.org/searches/reports/report/aacomas-applicants-and-matriculants-average-gpa-2016-2023); [AACOM MCAT report](https://www.aacom.org/searches/reports/report/applicant-and-matriculant-average-mcat-2016-2023)). 22,000+ DO applicants and ~9,600 matriculants in 2024.

**Women in 2024–25:** 56.8% of applicants, 55.1% of matriculants, 54.9% of total enrollment — i.e. women now numerically dominate the applicant pool ([AAMC 2024 data press release](https://www.aamc.org/news/press-releases/new-aamc-data-medical-school-applicants-and-enrollment-2024)).

### Post-SFFA changes (June 2023 onward)

The Supreme Court ruled race-conscious admissions unconstitutional in *Students for Fair Admissions v. Harvard / UNC* (June 29, 2023). Race cannot be a standalone factor, but applicants may "discuss how race may have affected his or her life, be it through discrimination, inspiration, or otherwise" ([Brookings analysis](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/the-complex-ramifications-of-students-for-fair-admissions-v-harvard/); [Wikipedia: SFFA v. Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Students_for_Fair_Admissions_v._Harvard)).

**Concrete admissions changes in the last 18 months:**
- **Essay prompt language shifted** from "diversity" to "experiences," "perspectives," "lived experience," "community" ([Inside Higher Ed coverage; MedEdits 2025 PS trends](https://mededits.com/medical-school-admissions/medical-school-personal-statement-trends-in-2025/); [MedSchoolCoach diversity essay guide](https://www.medschoolcoach.com/the-diversity-secondary-essay-for-medical-school/)).
- **Holistic review intensified, not weakened.** Many schools added or restructured rubrics to give more weight to narrative essays, life-experience interview questions, and CASPer/PREview professionalism signals.
- **Some declines in URM matriculation.** AAMC 2024 data: gains in URM *applicants* (Black +2.8%, Hispanic +2.2%) but matriculants declined for some URM groups — double-digit percent declines at some institutions ([JAMA Network Open on affirmative action repeal](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2839925); [AAMC 2024 press release](https://www.aamc.org/news/press-releases/new-aamc-data-medical-school-applicants-and-enrollment-2024)).
- **AAMC's official position:** Holistic review continues; race may be considered only insofar as the applicant raises it in their narrative.

**Practical implication for Maha:** Identity remains discussable when it shaped her — her Muslim identity, her Texas/UT background, her family's experience with U.S. healthcare — but it must be told as *story*, not as a checkbox. Schools are explicitly hungrier for narrative essays that show how lived experience produced clinical empathy and judgment.

### What "career-changer" narratives work vs. fail

**Working narratives (per [Shemmassian's non-trad guide](https://www.shemmassianconsulting.com/blog/non-traditional-medical-student); [MedSchoolCoach non-trad guide](https://www.medschoolcoach.com/non-traditional-medical-student/); [Leland's ex-AdCom PS guide](https://www.joinleland.com/library/a/medical-school-personal-statement-tips); [Accepted.com](https://blog.accepted.com/how-to-spend-the-gap-year-between-college-and-med-school/)):**
- "I always had a foot in medicine; the gap years were where I built the *clinical evidence* that medicine is the right fit." (Maha's lane.)
- Specific skill transfer: "Years of MA scope taught me X clinical insight that no traditional applicant carries."
- A turning-point patient encounter that re-framed motivation.
- Demonstrating commitment to *continue* (long longitudinal arc), not just *enter* medicine.

**Failing narratives:**
- Defensive timeline explanations ("I wasn't ready then...").
- Generic "I always wanted to help people."
- Framing prior career as something to escape rather than learn from.
- Specialty fixation pre-application (see Q6).

## Question 3 — The "what did you do with your gap years" filter

### Clinical-hours benchmarks

Sources cluster around the following ranges:

| Tier | Hours | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Floor for any competitive app | 100–150 | Most pre-health offices' published minimum |
| Solid traditional applicant | 200–500 | Common report from accepted students |
| Competitive non-trad | 1,200–2,000 | "Gap-year scribe = 2,000 hrs" benchmark |
| Heavyweight | 2,000–5,000+ | Multi-year MA/scribe; treated as employment, not extraordinary |

([Premed Catalyst on scribing](https://www.premedcatalyst.com/post/is-scribing-clinical-experience); [Stepful clinical hours guide](https://www.stepful.com/post/clinical-experience-for-medical-school); [International Medical Aid 2026 data piece](https://medicalaid.org/blog/how-many-clinical-hours-make-you-a-competitive-applicant-in-2026-a-data-driven-look/)). 

**Crucial nuance from multiple sources:** ADCOMs explicitly say *depth, continuity, and reflection* matter more than raw hour totals beyond ~1,500 hours. A multi-year MA at a real practice with leadership scope (Maha = "Lead MA") strongly outpaces a scattered shadowing portfolio at higher hours.

### Scribe vs. MA weighting

Scribe = documentation + observation; MA = direct patient contact + procedures (vitals, injections, biologic administration, biopsy assist). ADCOMs value MA scope *more highly per hour* because it demonstrates hands-on responsibility, empathy in real touch, and the ability to handle a real care relationship ([Adv Clinical comparison](https://advclinical.org/medical-scribes-vs-medical-assistants); [Med School Insiders evaluation framework](https://medschoolinsiders.com/pre-med/how-med-school-adcoms-evaluate-apps/); [Premed Catalyst clinical hours matrix](https://medicalaid.org/blog/the-pre-med-clinical-experience-matrix-what-medical-school-admissions-committees-actually-value/)).

**Maha's combination — 2 years scribe (pediatric ER documentation, physician-thinking exposure) + 3 years Lead MA (hands-on derm procedures + team leadership) — is among the *strongest* paired-experience profiles ADCOMs see.** The leadership title matters: "Lead" signals trust, longitudinal investment, and managerial scope.

### What else ADCOMs want to see

From the AAMC Premed Competencies (Q4) and ADCOM commentary: **leadership** (Lead MA — check), **service** (need a specific community-service longitudinal commitment outside paid roles), **scholarship/research** (her AI clinical tool counts as applied scholarship — see Q5/Q10), **longitudinal commitment** (multi-year continuity — check), and **reflection** (the personal statement and "Most Meaningful" essays).

`[JUDGMENT]` The portfolio site's most valuable role here is making the longitudinal arc *visually undeniable* in one screen — a timeline, role descriptions, concrete responsibilities, plus a "lessons learned" reflective gloss on each role.

## Question 5 — The AI-in-medicine angle

### What AAMC and academic medicine are actually saying

- **AAMC is actively developing national AI competencies** for medical education with Josiah Macy Jr. Foundation funding ([AAMC AI competencies page](https://www.aamc.org/about-us/medical-education/ai-competencies); [AAMC Artificial Intelligence Competencies for Medical Educators](https://www.aamc.org/about-us/mission-areas/medical-education/advancing-ai-resource-collection/artificial-intelligence-competencies-medical-educators)).
- **AAMC v1.0 Principles for Responsible AI Use in Medical Education** finalized January 3, 2025; **v2.0** July 31, 2025 ([AAMC Principles page](https://www.aamc.org/about-us/mission-areas/medical-education/principles-ai-use)).
- A 2025 AAMC framework "Could I, Would I, Should I?" provides a triage rubric for AI use in education ([PMC framework paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12360230/)).
- **AMCAS policy (2023–24 cycle and forward):** essays must not be written in whole or part by AI ([medRxiv prevalence study](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.10.21.24315868.full.pdf); [AAMC: AI will now read your application](https://www.aamc.org/news/ai-will-now-read-your-medical-school-application)). **Hofstra/Zucker uses an AI screener on ~5,000 applications/year**, trained on 22,258 historical apps with 88% test-set accuracy ([JAMIA Open 2023](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9936956/)).
- Multiple schools (Penn, Stanford, UCSF, Mayo, Duke) have published 2024–25 AI-in-curriculum policies; specifics vary. `[UNVERIFIED — I did not pull individual dean interviews for each]`.

### Is Maha's AI work a differentiator or noise?

**Differentiator, with caveats.** The signal is not "I am AI-savvy." Every applicant in 2026 will claim that. The signal is:
1. **A real artifact** — sohailmo.ai/mahaclinic is live, used by actual clinicians.
2. **Clinical-domain depth** — biologic dosing is FDA-regulated, error-prone, and benefits from a fast reference. This is a *patient-safety* tool, not a chatbot.
3. **Multi-site adoption** — used across multiple practice locations.
4. **HIPAA awareness** — assuming no PHI is stored (verify and state this prominently), this demonstrates clinical-informatics judgment.

**Risk:** Framed wrong, this reads as "tech-bro applicant who thinks she'll automate medicine." Framed right, it reads as "she saw a workflow gap during 3 years of MA work, built a tool that her physicians actually use, and learned firsthand the limits of AI in clinical settings."

**Recommended framing language (per [NEJM AI publications](https://ai.nejm.org/) — general editorial line, not a specific quote):**
- "Built to *reduce* physician cognitive load on a dosing-error-prone task."
- "Used by N clinicians across M sites since [date]."
- "Explicit guardrails: no PHI stored, no diagnostic recommendations, only an FDA-label and dosing-reference lookup."

`[UNVERIFIED]` I did not find a 2024–26 dean interview that explicitly says "we want applicants who build AI clinical tools." The AAMC competencies framework treats AI as *literacy*, not as an admissions-required experience. So this is a *strong elective signal*, not a *required competency*.

## Question 6 — Dermatology + primary-care framing

ADCOMs are skeptical of pre-application specialty commitments — especially to ROAD specialties (Radiology, Ophthalmology, Anesthesiology, Dermatology) ([MedEdits derm PS guide for residency](https://mededits.com/mededits-blog/residency-admissions/how-to-write-your-dermatology-personal-statement) — note: this is for *residency*, not med-school; applicants who fixate on derm pre-med-school often appear status-driven). 

### How to frame derm work without locking in a specialty

**Reframe the work as exposure to:**
- **Chronic-disease management** — biologics are for severe psoriasis, atopic dermatitis, hidradenitis suppurativa, etc. These are *systemic, lifelong, often disabling* diseases that overlap with rheumatology, immunology, pediatrics, and primary care.
- **Health-system inequities** — prior-auth burdens, insurance carveouts, biologic cost. This is *health policy and primary-care territory*.
- **Patient education** — biologic patients need ongoing education on injection technique, infection risk, vaccine timing. This is *primary-care work in derm clothing*.
- **Mental-health overlap** — visible skin disease + body image + depression/anxiety. This is *behavioral health territory*.

**Sample portfolio framing:** *"At Innovative Dermatology I led the medical-assistant team supporting chronic-disease patients on biologic therapy. The work spans patient education, insurance navigation, injection counseling, infection surveillance, and longitudinal follow-up — a chronic-care model I now see in primary care, rheumatology, and immunology."*

**Personal statement:** Do not name a specialty. Talk about *the patient*, *the system*, and *the moment that made medicine feel inevitable*. If the AI tool is the through-line, the through-line is "biology + technology + patients no one had time for," not "dermatology."

## Question 7 — Does a portfolio site actually matter?

### Honest answer

**Mostly, no — ADCOMs do not have time to visit applicant websites.** Holistic review already takes 60–90 minutes per file; reviewers work from AMCAS/TMDSAS/AACOMAS, secondaries, MSAR uploads, and the interview-day file. Standard advice (e.g. [AcceptMed 2025 digital portfolio guide](https://www.acceptmed.com/blog/building-a-digital-portfolio-for-med-school-applications-a-2025-guide)) suggests including a URL in the resume/PS *if* relevant — but warns that committees will not seek it out. The AAMC's [Medical Student Portfolios](https://www.aamc.org/media/22411/download) document is about *medical-student* portfolios for clinical-skill assessment, not pre-med marketing sites.

`[UNVERIFIED]` I did not find a specific 2024–26 SDN thread or AdmissionsDean episode that gives concrete data on what % of reviewers click applicant URLs. Best inference from cumulative content: low single-digit %.

### But the true audiences are real and matter

1. **Interviewers (night-before prep):** A clean portfolio that surfaces the AI tool, the timeline, and a short "why medicine" gives the interviewer something specific to ask about — converting an "okay" interview into a memorable one.
2. **LOR writers:** Her physicians need source material. A "for-letter-writers" page (private link) with bullet-pointed accomplishments, key patient stories (de-identified), and her role descriptions makes their letters concrete instead of generic.
3. **Post-interview thank-you / artifact follow-up:** "Thank you for our conversation. Here is the live tool I mentioned: sohailmo.ai/mahaclinic" is a *high-leverage* follow-up move.
4. **Secondary-essay self-reference:** A well-organized portfolio lets *her* find her own stories fast when writing 30+ secondaries.
5. **Networking (mentors, current students, residents):** A link makes her introducible.
6. **Identity / agency:** Owning a public-facing artifact has psychological value during a year of waiting.

### What content actually works (best-practice synthesis)

- One-screen homepage: who, why, the one artifact.
- Timeline of clinical roles (2017→2026), with concrete responsibilities not job titles.
- The AI tool: a live demo link + 3-paragraph "why I built this" + screenshots + a "guardrails / no PHI" disclosure.
- Selected patient stories (de-identified, HIPAA-clean) — 2-3 max.
- AAMC competency mapping (the table from Q4).
- A *quiet* "For interviewers" page (linkable but not advertised) with the timeline + tool + a "questions I'd love to discuss" list — confident but not presumptuous.
- A *private* "For letter writers" page with bullet-pointed accomplishments.
- *No* publication list bloat. *No* hobby photos in the lead. *No* GPA/MCAT printed publicly.

## Question 8 — CASPer, PREview, SJTs

### State of play

- **AAMC PREview**: 28 schools participating for the 2026 testing year; **10 require** it ([AAMC PREview participating schools](https://students-residents.aamc.org/aamc-preview/participating-schools); [Med School Insiders PREview list](https://medschoolinsiders.com/pre-med/what-schools-require-aamc-preview/)).
- **Casper (Acuity Insights' Altus Suite — Casper, Snapshot, Duet):** Used by 100+ programs in 2026 (medical, dental, PA, residency). Most U.S. MD/DO schools requiring an SJT use Casper rather than PREview ([Shemmassian on Casper/Snapshot/Duet](https://www.shemmassianconsulting.com/blog/casper-snapshot-duet); [Premed Catalyst Casper list](https://www.premedcatalyst.com/post/what-schools-require-casper); [BeMo Casper schools 2026](https://bemoacademicconsulting.com/blog/medical-schools-that-require-casper)).
- **Texas MD/DO schools (TMDSAS):** Most do not require either. UT Southwestern explicitly does *not* require Casper or PREview.

### What SJTs evaluate

Professionalism: ethics, communication, empathy, teamwork, equity, conflict resolution. Scenarios are video- or text-based; responses are short and time-pressured.

### How a portfolio that demonstrates clinical empathy helps

Indirectly. The portfolio is *not* visible to SJT graders. But the *thinking* a portfolio forces — articulating clinical-empathy moments, ethical reasoning, patient-systems failures — is *exactly* the rehearsal SJTs reward. Maha should write 6–10 short de-identified patient-encounter "stories" on the site (or kept private) and review them before any SJT or interview.

`[JUDGMENT]` Casper requires 14 minutes/scenario timed responses. Pre-writing reflective patient stories is the single highest-leverage Casper prep activity.

## Question 9 — Personal-statement themes hitting in 2025–2026

From [MedEdits 2025 PS trends](https://mededits.com/medical-school-admissions/medical-school-personal-statement-trends-in-2025/), [Shemmassian 2026 PS guide](https://www.shemmassianconsulting.com/blog/medical-school-personal-statement-analysis), [Leland ex-AdCom guide](https://www.joinleland.com/library/a/medical-school-personal-statement-tips), [ProspectiveDoctor non-trad example](https://www.prospectivedoctor.com/non-traditional-student-personal-statement-example/), [Ace Med Boards PS tips](https://acemedboards.com/sample-personal-statement-medical-school/), [MedSchoolCoach 13 PS examples](https://www.medschoolcoach.com/med-school-personal-statement-examples/):

### What works in 2025–2026

- **Open with a scene, not a thesis.** Specific moment, specific patient (de-identified), sensory detail.
- **Skill-transfer specificity** (for non-trads): "Years of MA work taught me X very specific thing that I now see is fundamental to physician work."
- **Systems failure → tool-building / advocacy** (this is *Maha's lane*): "I watched a 23-year-old psoriasis patient cycle through three biologics because no one in the practice could remember the contraindication chart in real time. I built the chart."
- **Reflective second-order thinking:** Not "I learned to be empathetic" but "I learned that empathy is *insufficient* without systems literacy."
- **Stake-claim, not status-claim:** Don't list awards. Show the cost / what you gave up / why it had to be medicine and not something easier.
- **Avoid:** specialty fixation, savior narrative, generic "I always wanted to help people," childhood-illness opener if it isn't the spine of the essay, AI-written prose.

### Three themes likely to land for Maha specifically

1. **"The chart no one had time to make"** — the AI tool as evidence of clinical observation + initiative + patient-safety motivation. Strong because it shows *what kind of resident she will be* without saying so.
2. **"The seven-year arc"** — owning the timeline as *intentional accumulation*, not delay. Frame the years as building three layers (pediatric ER → derm chronic care → tool-building) that converged on medicine.
3. **"My patients taught me what I couldn't learn in a classroom"** — the longstanding non-trad workhorse, made fresh by specificity. Avoid only if too generic.

## Question 10 — Niche validation

### The hypothesis

"MA + 5 yrs clinical + builds AI clinical tooling + Muslim woman from UT system + neuroscience grad" is a sharp differentiator.

### What can be verified

- **Women applicants:** 56.8% in 2024–25. Female + neuroscience-major + non-trad is not, on its own, rare.
- **Muslim:** AAMC does *not* publish religious-affiliation data. `[UNVERIFIED]` Best inference from Muslim-American population (~1.1% of U.S.) + medical applicant proportion estimates ~2–4%. Muslim women specifically are still a notable minority.
- **5+ years clinical at MA/scribe level + Lead title:** uncommon. Most non-trads have 1–3 gap years. 5+ years with leadership scope is in the top tail.
- **Published / deployed AI clinical tool:** very rare. AAMC does not track this metric. `[UNVERIFIED]` Reasonable estimate: well under 1% of matriculating MS1s have a live, multi-site-adopted AI clinical tool. Most "AI experience" listed on apps is course projects, hackathons, or research-assistant work. A live, in-use tool with multi-clinician adoption is genuinely uncommon ([general baseline from Stanford/Penn/Harvard student AI work — these are typically grad-student or post-MS1 efforts, not pre-med](https://www.aamc.org/news/ai-will-now-read-your-medical-school-application)).
- **Texas-system / UT Dallas + targeting Texas MD schools:** strong in-state advantage for TMDSAS schools (TCU, McGovern, UT Southwestern, Dell, etc.).

### Calibrated probability assessment

`[JUDGMENT]` Best estimate of applicants matching the *full* profile in a single cycle: somewhere in the range of ~50–150 (out of ~54,000+ MD applicants). Almost certainly under 200. This is genuine differentiation — not because each individual element is rare, but because the *combination* is, and because two of the elements (Lead-MA scope + live AI tool) are concrete artifacts that survive holistic-review skepticism.

### Where to push back on the hypothesis

- **Identity-as-differentiator is now more constrained post-SFFA.** Maha's Muslim identity helps *only as story*, not as category. The portfolio should support that storytelling — not foreground identity in a way that reads as identity-as-credential.
- **"AI builder" is becoming crowded fast.** Every cycle henceforth will have more AI-tool builders. The *enduring* edge is the *clinical embeddedness* (5 yrs MA + tool used by her own physicians) and the *patient-safety framing*. Lean into those, not into "AI builder" as a slogan.
- **MCAT and GPA still gate everything.** Differentiation is the cherry; the foundation is GPA 3.7+ and MCAT 510+ for most MD targets (lower acceptable for DO, TCU, mission-fit schools). No differentiation rescues a 3.3 GPA or 498 MCAT in MD admissions.

### Verdict

**Mostly validated.** The differentiator is real but narrowly so — it lives in the *combination* and the *artifact*, not in any single label. The portfolio's job is to make the combination *visible in 30 seconds* to anyone who lands on it.

## Implementation Recommendations for the Portfolio Site (Prioritized)

1. **One-screen homepage with the through-line.** Headline: not "Maha — pre-med." Try: *"Five years at the bedside. One tool my physicians actually use. Applying to medical school."*
2. **Timeline page (2017→2026)** that visually proves the 5-year clinical arc — pediatric ER → derm Lead MA — with one-sentence "what I actually did" under each role (vitals, biologic injections, prior-auth, supervising 3 MAs, etc.). This is the portfolio's single most useful artifact for letter writers and interviewers.
3. **A `/mahaclinic` deep-dive page** (the AI tool): "Why I built it" (≤300 words), "What it does" (≤200 words), "What it intentionally does NOT do" (no PHI, no diagnoses, no recommendations beyond FDA-label lookup), "Who uses it" (sites + clinician count if obtainable), live link, screenshots.
4. **AAMC Competency Mapping page.** A literal table mapping her 5 years to the 15–17 AAMC Premed Competencies with concrete bullets per cell. No applicant does this. ADCOMs notice.
5. **Patient Story Library (4–6 short de-identified vignettes).** Each: setting, what happened, what she did, what she learned. Doubles as Casper / PREview / interview prep. Mark `confidential, for portfolio-readers only` and keep HIPAA-safe.
6. **A "For Interviewers" page (linkable, not advertised).** Lists 5–10 conversation prompts she would love to discuss (the tool, the timeline, the specific clinical moments). This is generous, not presumptuous, *if framed as "I'd love to discuss these."*
7. **A private "For Letter Writers" page** (link-only, noindex). Bulleted accomplishments + her role descriptions + 2-3 concrete moments per recommender. Saves them time → produces better letters.
8. **Avoid:** GPA/MCAT in public, hobby photos in the lead, "I want to be a dermatologist," ChatGPT-flavored prose, religious imagery as decoration (vs. as part of a written story).
9. **SEO and discoverability are not the goal.** Don't optimize for search. *Do* make sure the URL is short, clean, and easy to say aloud in an interview.
10. **Add a quiet "Updates" log** showing she ships continuously — small entries (added a feature, presented at a clinic in-service, etc.). This is the cheapest way to show "Commitment to Learning and Growth" (AAMC competency #10) on an ongoing basis through the cycle.
